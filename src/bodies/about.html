Ordering from overseas, without the guesswork

Most of the friction in buying packaging from another country is not manufacturing — it is the parts nobody wrote down. These are the four that decide whether your order is straightforward.

  • Samples come first, always. A physical sample is produced before every production run and becomes the reference the whole order is measured against. Once the specification and artwork are fixed, samples take 5 – 8 working days; production then runs 12 – 20 working days from the later of the cleared deposit or your written sample approval.
  • Prices are quoted with the freight named. Quotations can be issued on EXW, FOB, CIF or DDP terms, so you are comparing landed cost rather than a unit price that hides the shipping. Sample parcels travel with DHL, UPS or FedEx, typically 3 – 6 days in transit.
  • Quantity changes the number more than anything else. Plates, cutting dies and machine make-ready cost roughly the same whether the run is 500 or 5,000, so unit cost moves most with volume. Rigid and magnetic boxes typically start in the low hundreds of pieces; simpler structures can go lower, and we will say plainly when a quantity is not economical rather than quote it as though it were.
  • Artwork that arrives late costs more than it saves. Print files, bleed and colour space are checked before anything is run; sending them with the enquiry rather than after the sample is the cheapest schedule insurance available.

What gets checked at each stage, and against which reference, is described on the quality control page. How the five sectors we most often build for differ is on the industries page.
